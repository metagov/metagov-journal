# The Institutional Catechism

This catechism provides the framework for documenting living institutions. Each submission must answer all nine questions to create a complete institutional specification.

## Purpose and Scope

The catechism structures institutional knowledge for peer review and reuse. Questions progress from foundational purpose through operational details to evolutionary dynamics. Authors should aim for 700-1000 words total, providing concrete examples and references throughout.

A "living" institution demonstrates:
- Active participation and ongoing operations
- Capacity for adaptation while maintaining core purpose
- Mechanisms for self-maintenance and reproduction
- Observable patterns of collective behavior

## The Nine Questions

### 1. What is the institution's purpose?

Describe the stable pattern of behavior this institution produces and maintains. What collective outcomes emerge from participant interactions? How do stakeholders recognize when the institution is functioning properly?

Consider:
- The recurring behaviors that define the institution
- Observable outcomes that persist over time
- Success indicators recognized by participants
- Boundaries of institutional scope

### 2. Who are the stakeholders and what are their roles?

Identify the categories of actors involved and their relationships. How do different stakeholder types interact? What roles are essential versus peripheral? How do stakeholders enter and exit the institution?

Consider:
- Core participant categories and stakeholder groups
- Role definitions and responsibilities
- Interaction patterns between stakeholder types
- Membership or participation criteria
- Accountable structures as which stakeholder have accountability for group beahvior
- Other Power relationships amongst stakeholder groups


### 3. What environmental factors shape the institution?

Describe the context within which the institution operates. What external forces constrain or enable institutional patterns? How does the institution interface with other institutions?

Following Lessig's framework, consider:
- **Law**: Formal rules and regulations
- **Norms**: Social expectations and customs
- **Markets**: Economic forces and incentives
- **Architecture**: Technical and physical infrastructure

### 4. What constitutes the institution's constitution?

Document the fundamental rules and structures that generate institutional stability. What formal and informal agreements govern behavior? How are these rules established, modified, and enforced?

Consider:
- Written documents (bylaws, terms of service, smart contracts)
- Unwritten norms and conventions
- Decision-making procedures
- Dispute resolution mechanisms
- Amendment or evolution processes

Provide sufficient detail that someone could understand or recreate the institutional structure.

### 5. Where are the field sites?

Identify specific instances where this institutional pattern can be observed. Where does this institution currently operate? What variations exist across different implementations?

Consider:
- Where is attention from stakeholders allocated
- where resources or tracked, assigned and distributed to/from
- Active deployment locations (physical or digital)
- URLs, addresses, or access points
- How these sites silod from and/or overlapping with each other
- Historical or defunct instances

### 6. What is your relationship to the institution?

Disclose your position relative to the institution being documented. How does your perspective shape this documentation? What aspects might you be unable to see or unwilling to share?

Consider:
- Your role(s) within the institution
- Duration and nature of involvement
- Potential biases or blind spots
- Access to information
- Motivations for documentation

### 7. How and when did the institution emerge?

Describe the institution's origins and initial conditions. What problem or opportunity motivated its creation? Who were the founders and what resources did they marshal?

Consider:
- Precipitating events or conditions
- Founding participants and their motivations
- Initial resources and support
- Early decisions that shaped development
- Evolution from founding vision to current form

### 8. How does the institution evolve?

Explain the mechanisms through which the institution adapts over time. How are changes proposed, evaluated, and implemented? What forces drive institutional evolution?

Consider:
- Formal change processes
- Informal adaptation mechanisms
- Response to internal tensions
- Response to external pressures
- Learning and knowledge accumulation
- Relationship between protocol and practice

### 9. What references support this documentation?

Provide sources that verify claims and enable deeper investigation. Include diverse evidence types: academic literature, primary documents, observable artifacts, and participatory venues.

Consider:
- Foundational documents
- Ethnographic studies
- Published essays or academic papers
- Public repositories or archives
- Community forums or discussions
- Media coverage
- Data sources

## Guidance for Authors

### Answer Completeness

Every question must receive a substantive response. If information is unavailable or restricted, explain why. Partial knowledge is acceptable if acknowledged explicitly.

### Evidence and Examples

Support abstract claims with concrete examples. Link to verification sources where possible. Show rather than tell how the institution operates.

### Appropriate Depth

Aim for 75-110 words per question as a guideline. Some questions may require more detail, others less. Total documentation should be sufficient for understanding and evaluation but not exhaustive.

### Living Institution Criteria

Throughout your answers, demonstrate that this institution is actively maintained and evolving. Historical institutions may be documented if their patterns remain observable in contemporary contexts.

### Reflexivity

Acknowledge limitations in your knowledge or perspective. Identify aspects requiring further investigation. Distinguish between observed facts and interpretive analysis.

## Using This Catechism

1. **Read all questions** before beginning to understand the full scope
2. **Gather materials** including documents, links, and examples
3. **Answer sequentially** as questions build on each other
4. **Review for completeness** ensuring all aspects are addressed
5. **Add references** to support claims and enable verification
6. **Acknowledge limitations** where information is incomplete

This catechism creates comparable documentation across diverse institutions while respecting their unique characteristics. The goal is not standardization but rather structured knowledge sharing that enables learning across contexts.
