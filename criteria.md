# Evaluation Criteria for Institutional Documentation

This document outlines the criteria used to evaluate submissions to the Metagov Journal. These criteria ensure documentation quality while respecting institutional diversity. This is a practical guide for aligning expectations amongst readers, authors, reviewers and editors regarding what is a quality submission at the principles level. The specific evaluation guide for reviewers can be found in evaluation.md.

## Core Evaluation Questions

### 1. Completeness

**Have all catechism questions been answered?**

Each submission must address all nine questions, though answers may vary in depth based on institutional maturity and available information. Missing answers should be explicitly acknowledged with explanations.

**Assessment Scale**:
- **Complete**: All questions answered substantively
- **Adequate**: Most questions answered, gaps acknowledged
- **Incomplete**: Significant questions unanswered without explanation

### 2. Directness

**Do the answers actually address the questions asked?**

Responses should directly engage with each question rather than deflecting or providing tangentially related information. It's acceptable to state uncertainty, but evasion suggests incomplete understanding or documentation.

**Warning Signs**:
- Answering different questions than asked
- Excessive abstraction without concrete details
- Circular definitions or tautologies
- Unjustified scope restrictions

### 3. Sufficiency

**Does the documentation provide enough detail for understanding and potential replication?**

Readers should gain sufficient insight to understand how the institution functions and potentially adapt its patterns. This doesn't require exhaustive detail but rather strategic completeness.

**Key Elements**:
- Concrete examples illustrating abstract concepts
- Specific tools and platforms identified
- Clear stakeholder roles and relationships
- Accessible references for verification

### 4. Explanatory Power

**Does the organizational description explain the institutional pattern's stability?**

The documentation should clarify how specific people, processes, and tools combine to produce and reproduce the observed behavioral patterns. The mechanism linking organization to institution should be evident.

**Evaluation Points**:
- Causal relationships identified
- Feedback loops described
- Sustainability mechanisms explained
- Evolution pathways indicated

### 5. Reflexivity

**Are limitations and unknowns explicitly acknowledged?**

Good documentation recognizes its boundaries. Authors should identify:
- Aspects not fully understood
- Areas requiring further investigation
- Potential biases in perspective
- Temporal limitations of observations

### 6. Verifiability

**Can claims be independently verified through provided references?**

Authors should distinguish between:
- Objectively verifiable facts (with sources)
- Direct observations (with context)
- Interpretations and analyses (with reasoning)
- Speculations or hypotheses (clearly marked)

**Reference Quality**:
- Links functional and accessible
- Sources relevant to claims
- Mix of evidence types
- Clear connection to documentation

### 7. Vitality

**Is this truly a "living" institution?**

Living institutions demonstrate:
- **Current Activity**: Active participants and ongoing operations
- **Adaptive Capacity**: Evidence of evolution while maintaining purpose
- **Viable Structure**: Mechanisms for self-maintenance
- **Future Orientation**: Plans or pathways for continued development

**Red Flags**:
- Only historical documentation
- No active participants identified
- Rigid structures without adaptation mechanisms
- Terminal or sunsetting states

## Applying the Criteria

### For Reviewers

Use these criteria to structure your evaluation:
1. Read the complete submission
2. Score each criterion independently
3. Provide specific examples supporting assessments
4. Offer constructive suggestions for improvements
5. Make clear recommendation

### Recommendation Framework

**Accept**: Meets all core criteria satisfactorily
- Complete or adequate answers
- Clear explanatory model
- Verifiable and living
- Minor improvements may be suggested

**Minor Revisions**: Strong submission with addressable gaps
- Most criteria satisfied
- Specific improvements identified
- Can be resolved in single revision cycle

**Major Revisions**: Promising but requires substantial work
- Several criteria partially met
- Significant gaps in documentation
- Requires comprehensive revision

**Reject**: Fundamental issues with submission
- Multiple criteria unsatisfied
- Not clearly a living institution
- Outside journal scope
- Insufficient documentation quality

### For Authors

Use these criteria for self-assessment before submission:
- Review each criterion honestly
- Strengthen weak areas
- Explicitly address limitations
- Provide strong evidence
- Test documentation with naive readers

## Special Considerations

### Emerging Institutions

Early-stage institutions may have limited history but strong vitality. Weight current activity and future potential appropriately.

### Traditional Institutions

Long-established institutions may seem static but demonstrate "living" qualities through gradual adaptation. Look for evolution over longer timescales.

### Failed Institutions

Documentation of defunct institutions may be valuable if:
- Failure mechanisms are analyzed
- Lessons for future institutions extracted
- Pattern variations explored

### Confidential Aspects

Some institutional details may be sensitive. Authors should:
- Indicate where information is withheld
- Explain confidentiality requirements
- Provide maximum possible transparency

## Evolution of Criteria

- Emerging documentation needs
